Reference 1-1

What is Phonetics?

The study of human speech sounds

2-1

Phonetics is the term used for the study of sound in human language (Mees & Collins, 2013). It focuses on the physical properties of speech sounds.

Sound Production

Phonetics is concerned with how sounds are produced, transmitted and perceived (Hamann & Schmitz, 2005).

Core Focus

In this study, we will specifically look at the mechanical production of speech sounds.

Production

Articulatory Phonetics

Speech is produced when air flows from the lungs through the vocal tract, which includes the vocal folds, nasal cavity, and oral cavity.

Airflow System

Air moves from the lungs through the vocal tract to generate sound.

Vocal Folds

Vibrate for voiced sounds but stay open for voiceless sounds.

Mouth Control

The main shaping system of speech sounds using tongue, lips, and jaw.

Transmission & Perception

Acoustic & Auditory Phonetics

After production, speech sounds travel as physical waves through the air and are received and interpreted by the human ear.

Sound Waves

Speech travels as vibrations through the air from speaker to listener.

Physical Signal

Measured in frequency, intensity, and waveform patterns.

Auditory System

The ear and brain interpret sound into meaningful language.

Visual Summary

How Speech Sounds Work

Speech sounds are produced when air moves from the lungs through the vocal tract. The tongue, lips, and vocal cords shape each sound differently, creating meaning in language.
